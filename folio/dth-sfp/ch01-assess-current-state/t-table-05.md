### Table 5. Common barriers to reporting SF medical products and potential digital solutions (pp. 12–13 / PDF pp. 24–25)

| Category | Challenges and barriers | Potential solutions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Health care professional capacity | • Health care professionals overburdened with work<br>• Time constraints<br>• Fear of repercussions | • Integrating app into existing workflow<br>• Automating data capture<br>• Minimizing manual data entry<br>• Reducing data requirements to the minimum needed for further analysis<br>• Protect the identity of reporter |
| Regulatory support | • Under-resourced NMRAs<br>• Lack of legal authority<br>• Lack of trained personnel | • Designing features for report processing, follow-up and feedback<br>• Providing training and resources<br>• Integrating NMRA feedback<br>• Legislating need for reporting by health care professionals |
| Health care system practices | • Polypharmacy, the use of multiple medicines by one patient, preventing health care professionals from pinpointing the effects of individual medicines<br>• Lack of continuity of care | While polypharmacy and lack of continuity of care are beyond the scope of reporting technology, the digital solution can be designed to account for these challenges by:<br>• providing detailed guidance on how to report suspect products in the context of polypharmacy<br>• designing the solution to be flexible and adaptable to different health care settings |
| Economic factors | • Economic and reputational damage for suppliers of having to recall medical products<br>• Cost of internet access and data | • Providing offline reporting options that can sync online when on Wi-Fi network<br>• Providing financial incentives for reporting |
| Social factors | • Fear of reporting due to reputational, professional or personal reasons<br>• Fear of legal consequences | • Providing options for anonymous reporting<sup>a</sup><br>• Incorporating regulatory and legal protection as part of reporting and investigative processes |
| Education | • Lack of awareness substandard and falsified medical products and how to recognize them | • Integrating guidance on substandard and falsified medical products detection within the reporting application |
| Technology | • Lack of digitalization of the reporting process, requiring health care professionals to use lengthy or paper-based reporting systems<br>• Lack of, or limited, connectivity | • A user-friendly reporting solution that is intuitive and appropriately automated<br>• Providing offline reporting options that can sync online when on Wi-Fi network |
